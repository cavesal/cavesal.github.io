First-trimester chromosomal screening

Risk cut-offs, nuchal translucency, secondary markers and management.

Own table CaVeSal® · Based on: Fetal Medicine Barcelona · Nicolaides KH 2005 (FMF)

2Management by risk — Fetal Medicine Barcelona cut-off

  • Very high (≥1/10): genetic counselling and offer invasive testing with microarray. If the woman prefers, NIPT may be accepted, unless there is an ultrasound anomaly or increased NT.
  • High (1/11–1/250): genetic counselling; NIPT or invasive testing according to informed choice (NIPT carries no miscarriage risk, detects ~99 % of T21, only the chromosomes studied; invasive testing carries a 0.1–0.2 % loss risk and studies all chromosomes). With an ultrasound anomaly or NT >p99: chorionic villus sampling + microarray.
  • Intermediate (1/251–1/1100): report the result and offer NIPT to complete screening. If NIPT is positive, confirm with invasive testing.
  • Low (<1/1100): give the result and explain the limitations of screening.

Own algorithm CaVeSal® · Based on: Fetal Medicine Barcelona — aneuploidy screening protocol

3Management by risk — Nicolaides 2005 (FMF) cut-off

  • High (≥1/100): offer invasive testing (CVS <14 wk / amniocentesis ≥15 wk) and genetic counselling. NIPT can now be offered as a non-invasive alternative.
  • Intermediate (1/101–1/1000): second-stage first-trimester ultrasound (nasal bone, tricuspid regurgitation or ductus venosus Doppler); offer CVS if the adjusted risk reaches 1/100 or more. NIPT is now an alternative.
  • Low (<1/1000): no further testing.

Own algorithm CaVeSal® · Based on: Nicolaides KH. Am J Obstet Gynecol 2005 (FMF)

4Nuchal translucency (NT)

  • NT is compared with the median and 95th percentile expected for the CRL, calculated as continuous curves between 45 and 84 mm: median from the Nicolaides 1998 equation and 95th percentile from 2.1 to 2.7 mm (Snijders 1998).
  • NT ≥3.5 mm: high risk regardless of age. Increased chromosomal and cardiac risk: refer to Fetal Medicine, CVS or amniocentesis, fetal echocardiography.
  • NT ≥p95 for CRL: increased chromosomal risk; offer NIPT or invasive diagnosis and fetal echocardiography.
  • Maternal age: age ≥35 years used to be a risk criterion. Today the FMF and Fetal Medicine Barcelona use the combined 1-in-N risk, which already includes age; that is why the calculator does not use it as a stand-alone cut-off.
  • Local references: population-specific ranges exist, for example Sharifzadeh 2015 (Iran, single centre), with a lower 95th percentile (1.8–2.35 mm). They are useful for comparison, but the screening standard remains the FMF.

Own algorithm CaVeSal® · Based on: Nicolaides KH et al. Prenat Diagn 1998 · Snijders RJM et al. Lancet 1998 (FMF)

6Ultrasound category and prior risk (ultrasound only)

CategoryCriterion
HighNT ≥3.5 mm (99th centile) · or NT ≥95th centile for CRL + 1 abnormal marker · or 2 or more abnormal markers
IntermediateNT between the 95th centile and 3.4 mm with normal markers · or normal NT with 1 abnormal marker
LowNT below the 95th centile and normal or not assessed markers
  • Markers: absent nasal bone, reversed or absent a-wave in the ductus venosus, tricuspid regurgitation. Frequencies in euploid vs T21: 2.5% vs 60%, 3% vs 66% and 1% vs 55% (Nicolaides 2011).
  • The cut-offs are those of the FMF: NT 95th centile by CRL (2.1 to 2.7 mm; Snijders 1998) and NT ≥3.5 mm (99th centile). The category is not a 1-in-N risk: for the combined risk use FMF-certified software or refer to Fetal Medicine.
  • NT centile: approximate. Up to the 95th centile it is computed with a log-normal curve (median from Nicolaides 1998 and spread derived from the FMF 95th centile); between the 95th centile and 3.5 mm, by linear interpolation.
  • Prior risk: risk of T21, T18 and T13 from maternal age and gestational age only, not adjusted by ultrasound (Hecht and Hook 1994; Snijders 1999). Example: age 35 at 12 weeks, T21 1/249, T18 1/580, T13 1/1826. With a previous trisomy, add 0.75%.
  • Maternal age is not part of the ultrasound category; it is shown separately in the prior risk.

CaVeSal® own algorithm · Based on: FMF — Snijders RJM et al. Lancet 1998 · Nicolaides KH et al. Prenat Diagn 1998 · Nicolaides KH. Prenat Diagn 2011 · Cicero S et al. UOG 2004 · Snijders RJM et al. UOG 1999 · Hecht CA, Hook EB. Prenat Diagn 1994

7First-trimester megacystis

  • 7–14.9 mm: aneuploidy risk ~20–25 % (T13/T18 more than T21): offer karyotype or NIPT. Ultrasound follow-up every 2–4 weeks; spontaneous resolution is common if isolated and euploid.
  • ≥15 mm: low aneuploidy risk if isolated, but ~90 % progressive obstructive uropathy (posterior urethral valves, more frequent in male fetuses). Refer to Fetal Medicine / fetal urology.

Based on: FMF/Nicolaides — first-trimester megacystis

8NIPT result

  • Positive NIPT: confirm with invasive diagnosis (amniocentesis or CVS), genetic counselling and referral to Fetal Medicine.
  • Negative NIPT: low but not zero residual risk; routine ultrasound follow-up.

Based on: Fetal Medicine Barcelona · FMF

2How first-trimester markers are combined

  • 2 or more high markers: high risk of early PE. Prescribe aspirin, serial PlGF and refer to Fetal Medicine.
  • 1 high + 1 moderate: moderate-high risk. Prescribe aspirin and reassess with 2nd-trimester uterine artery PI and sFlt-1/PlGF.
  • 1 high marker: aspirin according to the FMF combined risk; uterine artery PI check in the 2nd trimester.
  • 2 or more moderate: close surveillance, consider aspirin, reassess in the 2nd trimester.
  • 1 moderate: routine follow-up, integrating other clinical factors.
  • All normal: baseline risk.

Own algorithm CaVeSal® · Based on: FMF · Fetal Medicine Barcelona · FIGO 2019

3Aspirin to prevent preeclampsia

Aspirin ~150 mg every night from 11–14+6 wk until 36 wk, delivery or diagnosis of PE (FIGO 2019, regimen preferred by CaVeSal®). Alternative: 81 mg/day from 12–28 wk (ideally before 16) until delivery (ACOG PB 222, 2020).

Indications in the Calculator: high risk in first-trimester screening (uterine artery PI, PlGF, combination of markers) and pre-pregnancy obesity.

Based on: Poon LC et al. FIGO 2019 · ACOG Practice Bulletin No. 222 (2020)

4Ophthalmic artery — PSV2/PSV1 ratio

  • 1st trimester: complementary element of preeclampsia screening. There is no single validated cut-off and it does not indicate aspirin on its own.
  • 2nd/3rd trimester, with established PE/HDP — ≥0.78: suggests severe vascular involvement (PE with severe features). Integrate with BP, laboratory tests, sFlt-1/PlGF and fetal wellbeing.
  • <0.78: does not rule out severity; clinical judgement decides.

Own algorithm CaVeSal® · Based on: Gana N et al. UOG 2022;59:731-736 · de Oliveira CA et al. J Ultrasound Med 2013;32:609-616 · Diniz ALD et al. Int J Gynaecol Obstet 2008;100:216-220

5Uterine artery PI in the 2nd trimester

  • Enter the GA of the measurement: the Calculator reports the centile of the mean PI and compares it with the 95th centile for that GA.
  • >95th centile for GA: fetal growth surveillance and follow-up Doppler at 28–32 wk.
  • ≤95th centile: normal.

Own algorithm CaVeSal® · Based on: Gómez O et al. UOG 2008;32:128-132

7Mean arterial pressure (MAP)

  • MAP = (SBP + 2 × DBP) / 3.
  • MAP >90 mmHg in the 1st trimester: higher risk of PE; consider aspirin according to the combined risk.
  • MAP ≥105 mmHg: warning sign of gestational hypertension or PE, especially after 20 wk; assess proteinuria, sFlt-1/PlGF and liver and renal function.

Based on: FMF · WHO · NICE · ISUOG

Gestational age and biometry

CRL, 2nd- and 3rd-trimester biometry, humerus, cerebellum and estimated fetal weight.

1GA by CRL (1st trimester)

  • Robinson and Fleming formula (1975), the FMF standard, for CRL from 10 to 84 mm.
  • With GA by CRL the Calculator corrects the LMP and recalculates the EDD (corrected LMP + 280 days).
  • In the 1st trimester GA by CRL is the most accurate; if it differs from the LMP, ultrasound takes priority.

Based on: Robinson HP, Fleming JE. Br J Obstet Gynaecol 1975

2GA by 2nd- and 3rd-trimester biometry

  • Estimates GA with BPD, HC, AC and FL (Hadlock 1984) and compares the parameters with each other.
  • Difference ≤7 days: concordant biometry, symmetric growth.
  • 8–14 days: moderate discordance; monitor progression.
  • Mean GA: average of the parameters entered.
  • >14 days: significant discordance; the result shows which parameter is ahead or lagging.
  • Head ahead with concordant AC and FL: not an FL/AC asymmetry; correlate with dating, EFW and Doppler.
  • Lagging AC: consider asymmetric FGR (see the Growth tab). Lagging FL: assess humerus.
  • FL, AC and HC are copied automatically to the FL/AC Asymmetry tab.

Own algorithm CaVeSal® · Based on: Hadlock FP et al. 1984

3Humerus length and humerus/femur ratio

  • GA is estimated from humerus and femur with the Chitty and Altman (2002) equations.
  • Enter the GA by LMP or early scan to obtain the centile of each bone.
  • Humerus or femur <3rd centile for GA: short long bone; confirm dating, measure the other long bones, look for signs of skeletal dysplasia and refer to Fetal Medicine.
  • The humerus/femur ratio is reported as data, together with the expected value for GA; it does not define a diagnosis on its own.

Own algorithm CaVeSal® · Based on: Chitty LS, Altman DG. BJOG 2002;109:919-929

4Transverse cerebellar diameter (TCD)

  • GA is estimated with the Snijders and Nicolaides (1994) equation. The rule TCD (mm) ≈ GA (weeks) holds only up to 20–21 wk.
  • TCD is little affected by growth restriction: it helps confirm GA in FGR fetuses.
  • Enter the GA by LMP or early scan to obtain the centile.
  • TCD <5th centile for GA: suspected cerebellar hypoplasia; refer to Fetal Medicine and rule out Dandy-Walker and other CNS malformations.
  • TCD >95th centile: review dating.

Own algorithm CaVeSal® · Based on: Snijders RJM, Nicolaides KH. UOG 1994;4:34-48

5Estimated fetal weight (EFW)

  • Hadlock 1985: with Figueras, 4-parameter formula (BPD, HC, AC, FL) and, if any value is missing, the 3- or 2-parameter formula; with INTERGROWTH-21st, the 3-parameter formula (HC, AC, FL), the one that standard was built on.
  • Figueras 2008 (Fetal Medicine Barcelona): select fetal sex; if unknown, the average of both sexes is used. Valid 24–42 wk.
  • INTERGROWTH-21st 2020 (Stirnemann 2020): official equations for Hadlock EFW. Valid 18–40 wk (up to 41 if justified).
  • The formula and chart used are shown below the result.

2Macrosomia by estimated weight

  • EFW ≥4,500 g: severe macrosomia; planned caesarean recommended (ACOG/NICE). Shoulder dystocia risk 14–22 %.
  • EFW ≥4,000 g: close surveillance; shoulder dystocia risk 3–4 %. In diabetic women consider caesarean. Ultrasound check at 38–39 wk.
  • EFW 3,800–3,999 g: upper limit; serial biometry, especially in diabetic women.

Based on: ACOG · NICE · Fetal Medicine Barcelona protocol — macrosomia (Sep 2024)

Own algorithm CaVeSal® · Based on: Hadlock FP et al. AJR 1983;141:979-984

4Head circumference and GA in asymmetry

  • Exact HC centile by GA with the Chitty 1994 equation (derived HC): mean = −109.7 + 15.16·GA − 0.002388·GA³; SD = 3.913 + 0.2329·GA (mm, 12–42 wk).
  • Z-score = (HC − mean) / SD for the exact GA. Example at 32+0 wk: mean 297 mm, SD 11.4 mm; −2 SD ≈ 274 mm and −3 SD ≈ 263 mm.
  • Z <−3 SD: microcephaly, guarded prognosis; urgent referral to Fetal Medicine, neurosonography, rule out congenital infection (TORCH, Zika), chromosomal abnormality or genetic cause.
  • Z between −2 and −3 SD: suspected microcephaly; targeted neurosonography, assess proportion with AC and FL, follow-up every 2–4 wk. Most of these fetuses have a normal outcome.
  • Between −2 SD and P5: low HC (borderline); check in 3–4 wk.
  • P3 (Z −1.88) is the neonatal criterion used by the CDC during Zika; it is almost equivalent to −2 SD (P2.3). Prenatal ultrasound uses −2 and −3 SD (ISUOG; WHO 2016).
  • In asymmetric FGR or with uncertain LMP in the 3rd trimester, HC and FL are the most reliable parameters to estimate GA. In the 3rd trimester the LMP is not corrected: it is used as an approximate reference.

CaVeSal® own algorithm · Based on: Chitty LS et al. BJOG 1994 · Jeanty P 1983 · Cutoffs: ISUOG (microcephaly) · WHO 2016 · CDC (Staples 2016)

Advanced fetal and haemodynamic Doppler

UA, MCA, CPR by percentiles; ductus venosus, tricuspid, aortic isthmus and umbilical vein.

1UA, MCA and CPR by percentiles

  • Centile by exact GA with own equations fitted to the FMF model (Ciobanu 2019), range 20–42 wk.
  • UA PI >P95: increased placental resistance.
  • MCA PI <P5: cerebral vasodilation.
  • CPR: its percentile by GA is reported with own equations fitted to the FMF model (Ciobanu 2019); the P5 varies with GA (e.g. ≈1.38 at 29–30 weeks and ≈1.44 at 33 weeks).
  • CPR <P5: abnormal; cerebral redistribution, assess haemodynamic FGR. Confirm with a second measurement more than 12 h apart.

Own algorithm CaVeSal® · Based on: Ciobanu A et al. (FMF) UOG 2019;53:465-472

2Ductus venosus

  • Increased PI: venous dysfunction.
  • Absent a-wave: severe decompensation.
  • Reversed a-wave: severe decompensation; deliver.

Based on: Baschat AA. Obstet Gynecol 2011 · TRUFFLE Study Group. UOG 2020

3Tricuspid regurgitation

GradeManagement
AbsentPreserved fetal cardiac function.
MildClose surveillance; repeat Doppler in 48–72 h; assess DV, CPR and biophysical profile.
ModerateSignificant cardiac compromise; daily biophysical profile; consider delivery according to GA.
SevereSevere cardiac dysfunction: urgent delivery, referral to Fetal Medicine and neonatology.

Based on: Baschat AA 2011 · ISUOG Practice Guidelines FGR 2020

4Aortic isthmus

  • Normal PI: no advanced redistribution.
  • Raised PI: possible advanced cerebral redistribution; correlate with CPR and biophysical profile.
  • Reduced flow: onset of severe redistribution; intensive surveillance, repeat in 24–48 h.
  • Absent diastolic flow: severe haemodynamic compromise; imminent delivery depending on context.
  • Retrograde flow: maximal redistribution with left heart failure; urgent delivery.

Based on: Gratacós E, Torres X. Fetal Medicine Barcelona protocol 2023 · Arduini D, Rizzo G 1990

5Pulsatile umbilical vein

  • Mild pulsatility: intensive surveillance; repeat in 24 h; consider delivery according to GA.
  • Overt pulsatility: late fetal decompensation with venous congestion; urgent delivery coordinated with neonatology.

Based on: Baschat AA 2011 · TRUFFLE 2020

6Management with abnormal advanced Doppler

Coordinate with neonatology and refer to a tertiary centre. Timing of delivery depends on GA, biophysical profile and progression. For lung maturation and neuroprotection, see the cards Fetal lung maturation and Fetal neuroprotection with magnesium sulfate.

Based on: ISUOG Practice Guidelines FGR 2020 · ACOG Practice Bulletin 127 · ACOG 455

Fundal height and weight gain

Fundal height percentiles and pre-pregnancy BMI with weight-gain targets.

1Fundal height by percentiles

  • The Calculator compares fundal height with the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles for GA. McDonald rule of thumb: FH (cm) ≈ GA ± 2 between 20 and 36 wk.
  • <P10: rule out FGR, oligohydramnios or discrepancy with amenorrhoea. Ultrasound with EFW and Doppler (UA, MCA, CPR).
  • >P90: rule out macrosomia, polyhydramnios, gestational diabetes, undiagnosed twins, fibroids or GA discrepancy.

Own algorithm CaVeSal® · Based on: Gomes AG. RBGO 2001 · CLAP-PAHO/WHO

3Risks by pre-pregnancy BMI

  • Obesity: aspirin to prevent PE (see the Preeclampsia tab), early screening for gestational diabetes, BP surveillance and referral to nutrition.
  • Overweight: moderate risk of gestational diabetes, PE and macrosomia; GDM screening at 24–28 wk and BP surveillance.
  • Underweight: risk of FGR and preterm birth; calorie-protein supplementation and serial biometry.
  • Excessive gain: assess gestational diabetes and macrosomia. Insufficient: assess FGR, nausea or hyperemesis.

Mode-of-delivery prognosis

Probable Robson group, vaginal birth after caesarean and indications for planned caesarean.

1Probable Robson group

During antenatal care the Calculator assigns a probable group with the available data. The definitive group is assigned at admission for birth (WHO). For example, a nulliparous woman at term with a cephalic fetus is placed in group 1 assuming spontaneous onset; if induction is planned she moves to 2a and if caesarean is planned, to 2b. If birth occurs before 37+0 wk, she moves to group 10.

Only previous caesareans count: a myomectomy or other uterine surgery without caesarean does not change the group (WHO 2017).

The full classification and management by group are in the Emergencies guide: Robson classification and Management by group.

Own algorithm CaVeSal® · Based on: PAHO/WHO — Robson Classification implementation manual (2018) · FIGO 2025

2Vaginal birth after caesarean (VBAC)

  • Candidate: one previous caesarean with a low transverse scar.
  • Favour success: previous vaginal birth and non-recurrent previous indication.
  • Assess carefully: potentially recurrent previous indication; previous uterine surgery according to myometrial extent.
  • Absolute contraindication: classical or inverted-T scar.
  • Two caesareans: counselling by a senior obstetrician. Three or more: planned caesarean.
  • The Calculator estimates the probability of success with the Grobman score.
  • Requirements: immediate surgical availability and informed consent; discuss the risk of uterine rupture (0.5–0.9 %). Misoprostol is contraindicated.

Based on: Grobman WA et al. (NICHD) 2007 · FIGO 2025 · ACOG

3Indications for planned caesarean

  • Transverse or oblique lie (absolute indication).
  • EFW ≥4,500 g (ACOG).
  • Classical or inverted-T uterine scar.
  • Breech presentation: caesarean recommended unless external cephalic version is successful (offer ECV from 36–37 wk if not contraindicated).
  • Without a medical indication, planned caesarean is not performed before 39 wk.
  • Short maternal stature with high EFW: suspected cephalopelvic disproportion.

Based on: ACOG · NICE · PAHO/WHO Robson 2018

4Gestational age at term

  • Preterm (<37 wk): assess lung maturation and coordinate with neonatology.
  • Term (37–41+6 wk).
  • Post-term (≥42 wk): increased risk; consider induction and biophysical monitoring.

Bishop score: see the Emergencies guide — Mode of delivery.

Based on: ACOG Practice Bulletin

  • Grey zone (CaVeSal® suggestion): TSH >2.5 in T1 or >3.0 in T2–T3 and ≤4.0: check anti-TPO; if positive, consider levothyroxine.
  • History of the cutoffs: ATA 2011 and Endocrine Society 2012: 2.5 / 3.0 / 3.0 by trimester. ETA 2014: 2.5 / 3.0 / 3.5. ATA 2017 replaced them with the laboratory range or 4.0.
  • Trials: CATS (Lazarus 2012, 21,846 women, treatment at ~13 weeks: IQ at 3 years 99.2 vs 100.0) and Casey (2017, TSH ≥4.0, treatment at ~17 weeks: IQ at 5 years 97 vs 94) showed no neurodevelopmental benefit. The link between high TSH and cognitive deficit is observational.
  • Preconception: treated hypothyroid women, TSH between the lower limit and 2.5. IVF/ICSI with subclinical hypothyroidism: levothyroxine with a TSH goal <2.5 (ATA 2017, rec. 20).
  • Low TSH in T1: may be physiological due to hCG; confirm with free T4 and, if Graves disease is suspected, TRAb.
  • Low free T4 with normal TSH: isolated hypothyroxinemia; not routinely treated. Free T4 varies by method and trimester.
